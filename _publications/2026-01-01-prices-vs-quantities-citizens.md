abstract: 'We develop a theory of public appraisal to explain why similar carbon pricing instruments meet with different levels of public support. In a survey experiment with more than 13,000 respondents across seven European countries, we find that designing carbon pricing as "emissions trading" rather than "carbon taxation" reduces opposition in most countries, largely because taxes are more consistently perceived as costly. The results suggest citizens read taxes as the "tougher" instrument, and that emissions trading may be the more persuasive framing for constituencies not already inclined to support climate policy.'
